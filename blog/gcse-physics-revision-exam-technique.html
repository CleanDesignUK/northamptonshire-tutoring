GCSE Physics revision: equations, working and exam technique

Why GCSE Physics students lose marks, and how to practise calculations, explanations and required practical questions with an online tutor.

Published 11 November 2026 • 5 min read

GCSE Physics can feel different from many other school subjects because students need to explain ideas, remember key equations and use mathematics accurately. A productive revision strategy gives attention to all three rather than treating Physics as a list of facts to memorise.

1. Learn what the equation means

Simply memorising an equation is not enough. Students should practise identifying each quantity, checking units and deciding whether the equation is appropriate for the situation described.

For each formula, write one sentence explaining the physical relationship. For example, speed describes the distance travelled per unit of time. Then practise rearranging the equation and substituting values with units.

2. Write down your working

Physics examiners can award method marks even when a final numerical answer is incorrect. A clear calculation might include a chosen equation, a substitution, any rearrangement and a final answer with the correct unit. Check the specific mark scheme because requirements depend on the question and examination board.

Watch for unit conversions, such as milliamps to amps or centimetres to metres. Many errors come from inserting the correct number into an equation using the wrong unit.

3. Practise explanations using cause and effect

When a question asks you to explain why something happens, describe the science rather than repeating the wording of the question. Try breaking the answer into a cause, the scientific process and the observed result. Check the exact command word and number of marks.

It is useful to practise short explanations aloud before writing them down. If a pupil cannot explain an idea in ordinary words, they may need to revisit the concept before attempting a long exam answer.

4. Do not neglect practical work

Questions about practical methods, measurement, uncertainty, variables and graph interpretation can appear in GCSE assessments. Revisit the practicals covered in your specification and consider why each procedure was used.

  • Can you identify the independent, dependent and control variables?
  • Can you interpret a graph or an anomalous reading?
  • Can you suggest a practical improvement and justify it?
  • Can you explain what a measurement actually represents?

5. Use short mixed-topic practice

Revising one topic for an entire session can feel comfortable but may not prepare students to select methods from a mixed exam paper. After learning a topic, include a few questions from previously studied units so students practise recognising which method applies.

A simple revision routine is 10 minutes of recall, 20 minutes of practice questions and 10 minutes reviewing errors. More time can be devoted to topics that consistently cause difficulty.
