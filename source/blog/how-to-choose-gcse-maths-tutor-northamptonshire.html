Choosing a GCSE Maths tutor is not only about finding someone who knows the subject. The best match for a student in Northampton, Kettering, Corby or anywhere else in Northamptonshire is a tutor who can identify what is getting in the way and teach at the right pace.

1. Start with the specific problem

“Struggling with Maths” covers many situations. One student might know the methods but panic when a question looks unfamiliar. Another might need to revisit fractions and negative numbers before algebra makes sense. Before you enquire, write down two or three examples of questions your child could not complete independently.

This gives the tutor a clearer starting point than a target grade on its own. It also helps you decide whether to focus on rebuilding foundations, extending higher-tier skills or improving exam technique.

2. Check the GCSE exam board and tier

Ask your school whether the course is AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR or another specification, and whether the pupil is studying Foundation or Higher tier. Although there is shared content, the assessment structure and presentation of topics can differ.

Send the specification or a topic checklist if you have one. A suitable tutor should adapt examples and practice questions to the student's course.

3. Ask what the first lessons will look like

One-to-one online tutoring can be more targeted than simply working through a textbook in order. A productive starting sequence is:

  1. Look at recent work or a few representative questions.
  2. Identify a small number of priority topics.
  3. Model the reasoning behind a method.
  4. Let the student try comparable questions with feedback.
  5. Revisit the same skill later to check whether it has stuck.

For a student who finds fractions difficult, practising more algebraic fractions may not help until equivalent fractions, multiplication and division of fractions are secure.

4. Think about timing and consistency

One focused weekly session, followed by short practice between lessons, may be more useful than several rushed sessions immediately before an exam. The right schedule depends on the pupil's workload.

Because lessons are online, families in both larger towns and smaller Northamptonshire villages can avoid extra travel and ask about consistent times.

5. Ask these questions before booking

  • Do you teach this pupil's exam board and tier?
  • How will you identify the topics to prioritise?
  • Can you explain unfamiliar questions in more than one way?
  • How will you check that a topic is becoming secure?
  • What is the hourly rate and how do bookings work?
